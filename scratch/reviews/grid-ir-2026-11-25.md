# Top-level VDD/VSS grid: connectivity, IR and EM recheck (2026-11-25)

Power-integrity review, analysis only (no assembly code changed). Layouts:

- **pt_rf**: `scratch/integration/assembly-rf/chip/pt_chip_top.gds` (run 2026-11-25 08:53, flow at cee49db0: antenna diodes, MIM keep-out).
- **pt_wired**: `scratch/integration/assembly-wired/chip/pt_chip_top.gds` (run 2026-11-24 21:32, indicative: old macro, wrx_cap r6). A new
  pt_wired run was routing while this review ran, so its `masters.json` / `place.json` no longer match that GDS. The grid and macro
  results below come from the GDS alone and still hold. The pin-level checks and hook table for pt_wired are not given; rerun them on the new run.

Budget (MANDATE 5, spec/power-delivery.md): 117 mV static drop from the pads to each tap (pessimistic set, 85 C EM). EM limits: the tech LEF
`DCCURRENTDENSITY` (Metal1-4 0.67 mA/um, Metal5 1.5 mA/um, Via1-4 0.18 mA per cut). The 85 C values the power spec uses are 3.1x higher
(Metal2-4 2.09 mA/um, Metal5 4.5 mA/um, so about 0.56 mA per cut).

Scripts (in `scratch/integration/m1-pdn/`, run in the assembly tool image with `tool.sh <chip> python3 /repo/scratch/integration/m1-pdn/<script>`):
- `grid_conn.py` (KLayout connectivity: top-level Metal1-5 + Via1-4 and every block's own Metal4/Via4/Metal5; pad stubs and block supply pins are
  probed; knobs NOPINS / NOWIRE / EXCL / MACRONET);
- `grid_post.py` (classifies the floating pieces);
- `hook_em.py` (hook R, drop and EM from `route/routed.def`);
- `asm9_drawn.py regfile=` (the regional IR model fed with coverage counted on pad-fed metal only).

Outputs are in `out/<chip>/grid_conn.txt`, `grid_floating.json`, `grid_live.json`, `pinchk.txt`, `hook_em.txt` and `asm9_live*.json`.

| other | | within 0.1-2 points of drawn | |

pt_wired: SE 21.5 -> 16.9 % and MC 20.8 -> 16.5 % on Metal4; the rest is within 2 points.

**Real fault found on the way (not floating metal): the digital macro's own supply is not connected to the grid, on either chip.** The macro
(`pt_digital_macro`, DG9-28 on pt_rf) carries its PDN as 22 VDD + 22 VSS **vertical Metal4 stripes** (1.6 um, 76.8 um pitch). It has no Metal5
straps, and 0.35 mm2 of its own Metal5 is signal wiring. The top grid cuts Metal4 over the macro and runs Metal5 vertically, parallel to
the stripes, 2.5 um off the macro's Metal5. **Over the macro outline the grid has 0 Via4 cuts, and the stripes have 0 um2 of metal outside
the outline.** `grid_conn.py MACRONET=1` shows each stripe as an isolated net. LVS by name does not see this, because the macro's VDD/VSS
are not conn pins ("pin list from the conn only for digital"). As drawn, the macro's 60-82 mA has no path from the pads, so **neither die
would boot.**

### 2b. Hooks (`hook_em.py`, pt_rf routed.def): the dominant failure, not modelled until now

Every block supply pin reaches the grid through one router **hook**. A hook uses the HOOK rule (0.56 um wide on Metal2-5), has a 0.28 um
default-width taper at the pin and 0-14 single-cut vias. With the top wiring removed (`NOWIRE=1`), 243 block/net pin groups lose their
supply, so the hooks are their only path. Each island switch has **one** VDD hook carrying the whole island current:

| Instance / net | mA (design) | Hooks | Hook R (ohm) | Drop (mV) | x over LEF EM (narrowest wire / via) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| isw_lod VDD | 74.1 | 1 | 8.7 | **641** | 390x / 410x (1 cut) |
| isw_rf_rx VDD | 43.6 | 1 | 8.7 | 379 | 230x / 240x |
| isw_debug VDD | 40.0 | 1 | 32.6 | **1306** | 210x / 220x |
| isw_pll VDD | 30.7 | 1 | 4.6 | 142 | 160x |
| isw_rf_tx / _b VDD | 28.3 each | 1 each | 26.8 / 38.4 | 757 / 1085 | 150x |
| isw_lo VDD | 28.1 | 1 | 14.2 | 399 | 150x |
| isw_conv VDD | 18.7 | 1 | 22.6 | 423 | 100x |
| lo_txbuf VSS | 59.3 | 1 | 21.6 | **1279** | 160x |
| lo_mmd VSS | 34.8 | 1 | 13.9 | 485 | 90x |
| lna VSS | 21.4 | 1 | 83.5 | **1786** | 110x |
| rfdriver VSS | 24.3 | 1 | 28.5 | 693 | 130x |
| pll_vreg2 VSS | 20.9 | 1 | 13.6 | 283 | 110x |
| lo_ext VSS | 13.7 | 1 | 85.7 | 1174 | 70x |

Even at the 85 C limits every row is 10-130x over. The hooks would fuse or open at power-up, and before that each drops 0.1-1.8 V. That is
5-15x the whole 117 mV budget. pt_wired uses the same construction (one VDD hook per switch: isw_wired_cap 72.9 mA, isw_wired_tx_a/_b 65.7
mA each). Single-hook loads at 5-60 mA, which return through their VSS hooks, also fail. The `lo_vreg` row counts its tie-off pins as
hooks, so its split is optimistic. The hooks are also where the earlier asm9 runs were blind: they modelled the grid to the tap, not the
tap to the pin.

## 3. Fixes, ranked by risk

1. **Digital macro supply (dead die on both chips).** The preferred option is to re-harden the macro with routing capped at Metal4
   (RT_MAX_LAYER Metal4), so Metal5 over the macro is free. Assembly then draws **horizontal** Metal5 VDD/VSS straps across the macro
   outline: 10 um wide, 70 um pitch, alternating, joined at both ends to the vertical grid / core ring. Via4 arrays go at every crossing
   with a same-net stripe; a 1.6 x 10 um landing takes about 15 cuts (44 stripes x 19 rows). Cost: macro routing congestion, which digital
   must confirm. The other option keeps the macro as is: assembly places the same horizontal Metal5 straps only in the Metal5-free rows of
   the macro (0.35 mm2 of its Metal5 is signal), with each stripe needing at least 5 crossings over its 1333 um. The Metal4 stripe alone is
   75 ohm end to end. Either way, add a check that the macro's VDD/VSS stripes join the pad nets (for example `grid_conn.py MACRONET=1`, or
   LVS with the macro's supply pins), because LVS by name cannot see this.
2. **Island switch VDD feeds and heavy-load VSS returns: replace the hooks with drawn metal.** Extend the `ISLAND_BARS` mechanism to the
   supply side. Draw a Metal5 (+ Metal4) plate from each switch's VDD pin to the nearest VDD strap or the core ring, at least I / 2 mA per um
   wide at 85 C (37 um for isw_lod, 36 um for isw_wired_cap, 33 um for each wired_tx master). Use Via4 arrays of at least I / 0.5 mA cuts
   (about 150 for isw_lod) on full-width landings, and size the plate so it drops 10 mV or less at the design current (for isw_lod, 0.13 ohm
   or less). Give every load above 2 mA the same treatment on VSS: lo_txbuf, lo_mmd, rfdriver, lna, pll_vreg2, lo_vreg, lo_ext,
   vco_replica, adc_i/q, bbfilter_i/q, lo_obs/_q; on pt_wired, wrx_cap, wiredtx8, ptrim, pad_wire_tx, usb_txdrv and slicer. Where the pin
   faces a strap, the cheapest form is a **direct landing**: move the block or the strap so the strap covers the pin, as `direct_landings`
   already does for six atb / por pins. Small loads (1 mA or less) can keep hooks, but the hook rule must be at least 1 um wide with
   multi-cut vias (2 cuts per mA at the LEF limit). Block owners whose supply pin is a thin strip (0.8-1.6 um) must widen it for these
   landings.
3. **Grid metal where the islands draw (pt_rf ME, then SE).** Widen ME straps to about 12 % per net per layer (`strap_widths.json`
   ME). With SE also at 12 %, the worst tap is 111 mV (with SE alone at 8 %, it is 121 mV). Re-stitch the floating SE pieces round
   lo_lf / qvco (x 2844-3213, y 660-1223): 14 pieces of 5-16 x 10^3 um2, each cut on both ends, which need a Metal4 join or a Via4 to a
   live Metal5. On pt_wired, recover SE / MC (21 -> 17 %) the same way.
4. **Floating metal elsewhere: no action.** It is electrically harmless. Keep it as fill, or let the placer drop pieces under 20 x 10.5 um.

Fixes 1 and 2 are function-breaking (no boot; switch or load starved, then EM-open). Fix 3 is a margin item of about 15 mV. Fix 4 is
cosmetic. After fixes 1 and 2, rerun `grid_conn.py` (expect 0 floating pieces with pins and macro stripes on the pad nets), `hook_em.py`
(no hook above 1 mA) and `asm9_drawn.py regions=1 regfile=out/<chip>/grid_live.json`.
